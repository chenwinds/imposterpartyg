Imposter Game Questions & Clue Ideas

The hardest moment in the imposter game isn't spotting the liar - it's being put on the spot and having to produce a clue in five seconds. This page is a cheat sheet of clue styles and ready-to-use ideas, whether you're a civilian proving your innocence or an imposter bluffing for your life. New to the game? Read the full rules first.

The three tiers of clues

Tier 1 - Too obvious: "It's a yellow fruit." (The imposter now knows the word. You've lost.)

Tier 2 - The sweet spot: "It shows up at breakfast more than dinner." (Fits banana, vaguely fits other things. The imposter sweats.)

Tier 3 - Too vague: "It's nice." (Proves nothing. You look like the imposter.)

Aim for Tier 2: a clue that's clearly true for your word, but that requires actually knowing the word to produce.

Clue ideas by category

Food & drink words - clue the occasion, texture, or ritual, not the taste:

  • "Nobody's first choice, but everyone's third." (Tea)
  • "It arrives with its own little ceremony." (Sushi)
  • "The drive-through was invented for this." (Burger)
  • "Half the table judges you for ordering it." (Pineapple pizza - if it's in your word bank)

Animal words - clue reputation and habitat, not appearance:

  • "Internet royalty, zero respect for humans." (Cat)
  • "Smarter than it has any right to be." (Dolphin)
  • "Carries its house everywhere and still complains." (Turtle... or Tortoise - evil pair)

Place words - clue who goes there and why:

  • "Quiet is the whole point." (Library)
  • "You leave poorer but happier." (Mall)
  • "Sand in everything you own." (Beach)

Object words - clue the moment you reach for it:

  • "Only remembered when it's already raining." (Umbrella)
  • "Fights happen over who holds it." (TV remote - add it to your list!)
  • "Your phone made it obsolete, but you still own one." (Watch)

Questions to ask during discussion

The discussion round is where imposters actually get caught. Good questions force specifics:

  • "Describe your word in exactly three words."
  • "Would you find it in a kitchen? Answer yes or no, no explaining."
  • "Say your clue again, but slower."
  • "Who did you suspect first, and why?" (Turns the heat on aggressive accusers - a classic imposter move.)

How to spot the imposter's clue

  • The echo: their clue is a remix of the previous player's.
  • The hedge: "It's kind of... a thing you... use?" Confidence gaps betray bluffers.
  • The overfit: their clue fits every word on the table. "It's fun!" helps nobody.
